# Post-hoc scientific integrity review: regional A/B covariance

**Status:** analysis-in-progress, non-destructive; **NOT** an independently confirmed Planck effect.  
**Original published baseline:** [CMB–CST Lab](https://github.com/ZuluYokohama/cmb-cst-lab), `results/spatial.json`.  
**Stage:** After the 2026-10-22 Stage A blind unfreeze. This audit cannot be called preregistered.

## Why this review exists

The current `edge_constancy` routine in `src/cmbcst/spatial.py` sums independent inverse-variance constancy tests for four same-frequency A/B relative-angle edges. It assumes their **errors are independent of one another** within each sky region and independent **between regions**.

The four detector-map edges use disjoint detector-map labels, but they observe a shared sky. Disjoint map labels **alone** do not rigorously prove independent edge-estimator errors after processing, foregrounds, correlations, filtering, masking and nonlinear pair-angle fitting. The main pipeline estimates a full covariance across its pair-angle measurements, so the covariance cross-terms should be inspected rather than assumed zero.

The public repository currently reports:
- **Primary 12-region** A/B result: `chi2=45.8, dof=44, nominal p=0.397`.
- **Secondary 8-band** A/B result: `chi2=49.0, dof=28, nominal p=0.0083`.

These are **the existing diagonal-approximation outputs**, not results from the new joint-covariance test. They should not be interpreted as independently established evidence of a physical nonstationarity, especially because the primary test is not significant at the registered `p<0.01` decision rule and the 8-band result is a secondary robustness analysis.

## Implemented audit

- `src/cmbcst/spatial_ab_audit.py` provides a GLS constancy test that keeps the full `4x4` covariance *within each region*. It profiles the four constant edge angles in a simultaneous fit.
- `tests/test_spatial_ab_audit.py` compares it to the original method for diagonal covariance, an independently whitened stacked GLS implementation, and an adversarial correlated-Gaussian null.
- `scripts/audit_spatial_ab.py` reads the locally cached regional pseudo-spectra, regenerates pair-angle estimates, confirms the old diagonal statistic matches the already-published `results/spatial.json`, and writes a **private, never-overwritten, expressly post-hoc** joint statistic and source-file hash list outside the repository. No Planck result is invented or generated by the present pull request.

### Statistical point

With four pair-angle errors that are pairwise correlated at `rho=0.8` within independent regions, summing four univariate chi-square statistics can have a nominal 5%-tail false-alarm rate substantially above 5%. An illustrative fixed-seed simulation gives approximately **14%**, while the properly modeled joint GLS test gives approximately **5%**. This is a counterexample to the *assumption*, **not** evidence that the actual Planck pairs have `rho=0.8`.

The joint GLS test remains conditional on an assumed **block diagonal covariance between sky regions** and treats covariance estimates as fixed. It does not correct map-level cross-region covariance, beam/foreground leakage, Wishart vs pixel likelihood differences, or look-elsewhere corrections.

## Independent validation required

1. Execute the post-hoc script on the original local cached Planck regional spectra; preserve hashes of those inputs and the original frozen result. Never present new estimates as preregistered.
2. Inspect the actual empirical within-region `4x4` covariance matrices and differences between diagonal and joint statistics. Test `C_r` conditioning and stability.
3. Build enough **map-level** synthetic sky runs with the actual masks, beam and foreground/noise systematics to estimate **between-region cross covariance** as well as within-region cross-edge covariance. Forty map-level simulations are insufficient to characterize high-dimensional cross-covariance precisely.
4. Calibrate null tail probabilities empirically on independent held-out skies with finite-sample p-value corrections. Predefine the test family before another genuine blinded dataset or partition is inspected.
5. Separate the 12-patch primary and eight-band secondary result; adjust for repeated partitions, metrics and diagnostic exploration.
6. Acquire outside review of claims, covariance approximations, data lineage, and reproduction conditions.
